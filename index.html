The shift on the island

The Thai Government Is Cracking Down on Nominee Companies

FIELD NOTE 01

Plenty of land for sale. Almost none of it for lease.

Foreigners can't directly own land in Thailand, making the ownership structure critical. A compliant long-term lease is one legal option. Another is a Thai company with genuine Thai ownership—not nominee shareholders. As scrutiny of nominee structures increases, more owners are forced to sell. The result: more land entering the market for sale, while properly structured lease opportunities remain extremely limited.

FIELD NOTE 02

Regulatory pressure is creating buying opportunities.

As enforcement around nominee structures increases, some property owners are deciding to exit sooner than planned. In certain cases, that leads to faster sales and more negotiable pricing. For buyers with a properly structured, fully legal ownership setup, it can create opportunities that are difficult to find in a more balanced market.

The dream of owning land here is real. So is the fear of getting the legal setup wrong. Leasehold is the one route that's completely clean — almost no one is offering it well. That's the gap Sure Phangan was built to close.

How we close it

We create the leasehold opportunities this market is missing — at prices below where they started.

First, we create the legal route itself. Since foreign buyers can't hold title directly and almost everything on the market is being sold outright, we structure the one path that's fully compliant — a professionally documented long-term lease, no nominee company, no grey zone.

Second, we create the pricing. Because these landowners are selling under real pressure, we're able to negotiate meaningfully below asking price — and pass that advantage on rather than pricing it in.

In many cases, Sure Phangan acquires a larger parcel of land, subdivides it into smaller plots, and offers each one through its own long-term lease. That makes ownership far more accessible, allowing you to secure your own piece of Koh Phangan without the cost of purchasing an entire parcel.
